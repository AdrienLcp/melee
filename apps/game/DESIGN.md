# Design System: Melee

Recorded from the world built in stage 29 (`docs/plans/29-redesign.md`), not
from the intention that preceded it. The tokens live in
`src/presentation/styles/_tokens.sass`; where this file and that one disagree,
the code is right and this file is stale.

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Game Box on the Table"**

Melee is a shelf of game boxes on a table, and every screen is a printed
piece out of one of them: the matte board everything is laid on, paper cards
for what is read, die-cut chipboard pieces for what is pressed, a pawn per
seat, and a spine per game on the shelf. Nothing is an app panel — no rounded card floating on a dark ground,
no glow, no gradient. The board is pale sky by day and slate blue by evening;
the printed pieces stay printed when the lights go down.

The world was chosen by Adrien on 2026-10-04 from three example pages
(`.impeccable/directions/boite.html`, local and uncommitted, is the reference
the finish is judged against). Two looks are ruled out by `PRODUCT.md`: the
party-game app (near-black, one neon accent, glows) and any period costume.

Density follows the reader. The console is read by a group from up to four
metres, so it carries one idea at a time and lets it grow into the field; a
player's screen is read at forty centimetres in glances, and the round on it
never scrolls.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One flat board colour on every screen at every phase; the phase is said by
  the ink of the round's own marks — its sand and its pips.
- Every control is a die-cut piece: a 3px chipboard edge, sprue nubs, lift
  under a pointer, sink under a press.
- Four answer inks, each with a printed shape, so colour is never the only cue.
- Twelve pawn colours and three heads; the same piece names the same player
  on every screen, and no two seats ever hold the same one.
- Two faces: Bricolage Grotesque condensed for what is printed on the box,
  Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for what is read.
- A question and its four choices never scroll, measured at sixteen viewports.

## Colors

A cool printed board under warm, saturated die-cut inks, every value one
`light-dark()` pair read off `color-scheme` (set by
`@adrienlcp/theme-preference/color-scheme.css`).

### Primary
- **Go Coral** (`--primary`, edge `--primary-edge`, label `--on-primary`): the
  one colour that says *go* — the `filled` button, the player's buzzer. Same value in both palettes; its darker edge is the piece's
  chipboard.

### Secondary
- **The four answer inks** (`--tile-1` coral, `--tile-2` saffron, `--tile-3`
  sky, `--tile-4` leaf), in dealing order, each with its shape mark ink
  (`--tile-mark-1..4`) and one text ink (`--tile-ink`). Saffron `--tile-2` is
  also the room-code token and the slate's *collected* token — the one saffron
  piece on the board.
- **Spines** (`--spine-blindtest` magenta, `--spine-quiz` indigo,
  `--spine-buzzer` vermilion — deeper and redder than the coral `--primary`
  it once equalled, so the one press that says *go* is never read as a game's
  box — `--spine-reflex` saffron, `--spine-slate` teal,
  `--spine-atlas` leaf, `--spine-challenge` walnut — the game played at the
  table, off every screen, in the table's own wood, and far enough from magenta
  and indigo that the three never read as one family; `--spine-innerclock`
  dusk — a muted night blue for the game played in silence, dark enough
  beside the light sky tile and grey enough beside the indigo that neither
  reads as its family; `--spine-matchsticks` plum — a matchbox, far enough
  round from indigo and magenta to sit between them; `--spine-timeline`
  bronze — an old atlas's olive-gold, halfway round from walnut to leaf so it
  reads as neither; `--spine-blacksquare` wine — an ink-black spine vanishes on
  the dark board, and every other free hue sits within twenty degrees of a
  neighbour's; `--spine-petitbac` violet — a school ink, the colour of the pen
  the paper game was played with, twenty degrees short of the plum and as far
  again past the quiz's indigo): each
  game's box colour, on the shelf, the band across a game's lid, the player's
  *up next* lid. Names print in `--on-spine-<game>`: pale `--on-spine`
  everywhere but reflex, which takes the dark `--on-spine-light`.

### Tertiary
- **Pawns** (`--pawn-1..12`). Twelve because the four added to the original
  eight — lime, brown, magenta, sky — keep the closest pair in OKLab as far
  apart as the eight's closest already was (green / teal, 10.8); a thirteenth
  hue fell under it. Colour-blind simulation already merged pairs among the
  eight (pink / teal under protanopia), so a pawn is never told apart by its
  colour alone: a team is named beside its pawn, and a seat's piece wears a
  **head** beside its colour — round, square or pointed (`PAWN_HEADS`).
  Twelve colours times three heads is thirty-six pieces for at most
  twenty-four seats, so every seat holds a piece nobody else holds and twelve
  are always free to swap into. An arriving seat takes the piece its device
  asks for when it is free, else the colour the fewest seats hold, lowest
  first, in its first free head (`pawnPieceForArrival` in
  `@melee/core/board/pawn-piece`): a table of twelve stands in twelve round
  heads, and the thirteenth seat is the first square one. A team's pawn always
  wears the round head. A pawn's colour is never a ground text is printed on.

### Neutral
- **Board** (`--board`, `--board-ink`, `--board-muted`): the ground of every
  screen. Components read the aliases `--field`, `--ink`, `--ink-muted`, which
  point at the board on `:root` and are re-pointed by a lid or a flip.
- **Printed blues** (`--track-a`, `--track-b`, `--track-five`, `--track-ink`),
  named after the score track they were printed for and kept when it went.
  `--track-a` is the face of an `outlined` button and a roster chip;
  `--track-five` / `--track-ink` are the phase ink at rest.
- **Paper** (`--card`, `--card-ink`, `--card-muted`, `--card-rule` the keyline
  inside a lid): question cards, the QR card, cover mounts, notices, text
  fields. Paper stays light in the dark palette and its ink is one value in
  both.
- **Chipboard** (`--chipboard`, `--chipboard-dark`) and **`--shadow-ink`**: the
  raw edge under a piece's print, and the colour of the soft fall under it.
- **Selection** (`--selection`): a tint of the track's blue that the board's ink
  reads on, so selected text never shows the browser's own colour.
- **Socket** (`--socket`, `--socket-line` the dashed die-line, `--socket-ink`
  what is printed at the bottom of a recess, such as a marked slate item's
  numeral): the recess a piece sits in when it is absent, spent or disabled.
- **Derived lines**: `--rule` (22% ink into the field) separates regions and
  owes no contrast; `--rule-strong` (60%) is a control's own boundary and owes 3:1
  (WCAG 1.4.11).

### Feedback
- **Good** (`--good`, `--good-edge`, `--good-soft`): a right answer, the
  *revealed* phase, the payout token.
- **Danger** (`--danger` ground, `--danger-ink` text): always a stamped block,
  never tinted text, so its contrast is fixed in both palettes.
- **Focus**: no colour of its own. The ring is drawn in `--ink`
  (`_focus.sass`), the one colour guaranteed against whatever ground is current,
  since the phase and the reflex flip repaint the ground under it. Every
  focusable rings by default through `focus.ring-focusables` in `globals.sass`;
  a component includes `focus.ring` only where its ring differs.

### The phase ink

`usePhaseField` stamps `data-phase` on `<html>`; `_tokens.sass` turns it into
`--phase-ink` / `--phase-on-ink`, which the round's own marks read — the
draining sand, the current pip, the hold groove under the console's stage:

| Phase | `--phase-ink` |
|---|---|
| `lobby` | `--track-five` — the printed blues |
| `countdown` | `--spine-reflex` (saffron) |
| `playing` | `--spine-quiz` (indigo) |
| `buzzed`, or `playing` while the slate is marking (`data-marking`) | `--primary` (coral) |
| `revealed` | `--good` |
| `finished` | `--track-five` |

`data-flipped` (the reflex stimulus, `use-flip-field.ts`) swaps `--field` and
`--ink`: the largest luminance jump either palette holds, at the cost of no new
colour.

**The Board Never Repaints Rule.** The ground is the board at every phase. A
phase changes the marks' ink, never the room's colour; the flip is the only
time the field changes, and it changes by inversion.

**The Printed Piece Rule.** Paper, tiles, spines and pawns are the same in both
palettes (or shift a few points of lightness): a card does not change colour
when the lights go down. Only the board, its printed blues, socket and chipboard follow the
scheme.

**The Shape Beside Ink Rule.** No two things are told apart by hue alone: tiles
carry a shape, a seat carries a name beside its pawn, a state carries a word.

**Contrast, measured.** `tokens-contrast.test.ts` checks every ink on the ground
it is printed on, in both themes: every text pair clears 4.5:1 (worst: the
muted board ink on the dark `--track-a`, 5.93:1) and every shape mark 3:1 (worst
3.37:1, on the dark coral).

## Typography

**Display Font:** Bricolage Grotesque (`--font-display`, width axis 75–100%,
weight 200–800), falling back to Atkinson Hyperlegible Next
**Body Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (`--font-base`, weight 200–800), then
the system stack

**Character:** Bricolage, condensed, is the lettering printed on the box — a
lid title, a room code, a score, every figure. Atkinson is everything read up
close, chosen for the grandparent at the table. Both are SIL OFL, self-hosted
in `public/fonts`, subset latin / latin-ext, each latin face preloaded at the
default priority by each prerendered page — party Wi-Fi is the reason for all
three — and `font-display: swap`, because a player joins on `/play/…`, which is
the SPA shell: no preload, stylesheets injected by JavaScript, and an
`optional` face there missed its window on every phone for the whole evening. Each face has a fontaine fallback scaled to
its metrics, listing Arial and its metric twins (Liberation Sans, Arimo, Roboto)
so the swap moves no line on Linux or Android either; `font-synthesis: none`
keeps a weight the face lacks from being faked.

### Hierarchy

The mixins live in `src/presentation/styles/_typography.sass`, and they hold
every weight, leading and tracking in the game: a component includes one and
sets at most its own size. Sizes grow with `--stage-unit` (`1vmin`), the one
unit through which the game opts out of sizing on its container: the same page
is read at forty centimetres and at four metres, so a phone upright and a
television lying down both land on something readable, and every clamp has a
rem floor and a ceiling so a projector stops it growing.

**Past a short side of 1080px, `rem` itself grows** (`font-size: max(100%,
100vmin / 67.5)` on `html`): a 4K panel at 100% scaling is a 1080p one with
smaller pixels, read from the same sofa, so it draws exactly the 1080p stage at
twice the size instead of floating 1080p's ceilings in the middle of the wall. A
media query in `rem` still reads the browser's own size; a `px` border does not
grow, which is the one thing a 4K wall draws thinner.

- **`monument`** (800, stretch 80%, `opsz` 96, line-height 0.9): the room code,
  the countdown numeral.
- **`billboard`** (800, stretch 85%, line-height 0.95): a revealed title, the
  name of whoever buzzed, a winner, a notice's title. A line with a French
  grave (`à`) set in it needs line-height 1.2: the accent rises past 0.95.
- **`title`** (700, stretch 90%, line-height 1.05): control labels, roster
  names, and every block's ruled heading (`SectionHeading`): *Cette manche*,
  *Classement général*, *Classement final*.
- **`body`** (400, line-height 1.45): the default on `<body>`.
- **`caption`** (500, line-height 1.35): reasons, details, strip segments.
- **`label`** (700, tracked 0.005em, sentence case): form labels, menu items.
  Never tracked capitals — at 12px they are the hardest line on any screen.
- **`numeric`** (Bricolage 800, tabular figures): every score and count, so a
  score going from one digit to two does not jolt the row.

`body`, `caption` and `label` carry a `--stage-unit` term because a player's
column widens from 343 to 828px and the faces above them double over the same
span, so a flat `rem` there is the phone screenshot enlarged that the `screen`
mixin already refuses one level up. Measured on the player's lobby at 1440×900:
`blindtest.name` went 35px → 72 while the French `player.upNext` held 12,
taking a ratio of 2.9 to 6. Their floors are the phone values, so a viewport
whose shorter side is under 750px renders exactly as it did; their ceilings
land between a shorter side of 875 and 900px, where the column stops widening —
the two grow together, and then both stop.

Two trims from `presentation/styles/_text-box.sass`, both guarded by
`@supports (text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic)`: `trimmed-block($padding-block)`
for a painted block of short text (a stamp, a segment, an error), whose padding
grows by what the trim takes so nothing measured against it moves;
`trimmed-figure` for a figure on its own line. A board that sizes its type by
dividing a height by its rows takes a smaller divisor under the same guard —
`--standings-row`, `--tallest-row`, `--board-row` — measured on rendered rows.

**The question** is sized by the square root of its length:
`clamp(var(--text-xs), min(k / sqrt(length), cap × yield), cap)`
(`_question-card.sass`), the caller setting `--question-k`, `--question-cap` and
`--question-yield` so a question yields room to long choices. Answer tiles and
every board fitted to its box size the same way. `--text-xs` is 0.875rem —
14px at the default size, the floor the e2e spec enforces.

**The No Kicker Rule.** No label sits above a heading to frame it. Every kicker
the old world had — *La réponse*, *Tu vas jouer à*, *Tu finis*, *En ce moment
au mur*, the console reveal's title prefix, the reflex time's label — became a
`VisuallyHidden` prefix of the heading it framed, so a screen reader still hears
the sentence and the eye lands on the payload. The final board's *WINNER* went
outright: the name never needed it.

**The Rubric Exception.** The quiz category stays visible above the question
(`.category`, `order: -1`, `--tile-mark-3` ink, on the question card): it is
content the table reads aloud, not a label restating the heading.

**The Typed Text Wraps Rule.** A nickname, title or search set at `billboard`
or above takes `overflow-wrap: anywhere` inside a parent that will not grow
(`min-width: 0` is half the fix). A fixed-height row ellipsises instead.

## Layout

**One split, at 56.25rem** (900px, `$wide-screen`, from `@adrienlcp/styles/breakpoints`,
forwarded by `_layout.sass` with `wide` and `narrow`). A second width,
`$poster-screen` 75rem (1200px), is where a front door splits into poster and actions
(`poster`, columns 1.35fr / 1fr).

**Three page shapes** (`_layout.sass`, `_framed.sass`):
- **`screen`** — the player's column: 38.75rem (`--column-max-width`), widening
  to 56.25rem past the split, gap `--space-l`. It widens because the type grows
  with `--stage-unit` and the measure has to grow with it.
- **`stage`** — the console and the wall: the whole field to 112.5rem
  (`--stage-max-width`), never centred in a column.
- **`framed.page`** — a screen held to `100dvh` with `overflow: hidden`: the
  player choosing, the seated host choosing, the player's buzzer. A grid of
  chrome / round, and a size container (`choosing`) the round's queries read.
  Upright: four tile rows, 2×2 when the box is ≥560px wide. Lying down
  (`min-aspect-ratio: 13/10`): the chrome and the question card on the left,
  the tiles the whole right column (0.82fr / 1.18fr), the menu moved to the
  line's start. Choices over 60 characters (`data-long-choices`) are four
  full-width rows at every ratio and widen the right column to 1.45fr.

**Padding** is `--layout-padding` (`clamp(1rem, calc(var(--stage-unit) * 4), 3.5rem)`), resolved per
side against the safe-area insets (`--layout-padding-block-start` etc., `max()`
of the two over the `--safe-area-*` tokens of `tokens.defaults`, which carry the
`0px` fallback and read Android's static `safe-area-max-inset-bottom` first).
`#root` is not padded: every surface that reaches an edge pays the inset once,
through these. A framed screen uses the tighter
`--framed-padding-block-*` (`clamp(var(--space-xs), calc(var(--stage-unit) * 2), 1rem)`).

**Console stages are size containers.** Above the split each console stage
(playing, reveal, countdown, floor, final, slate, reflex) is a three-row grid —
header, stage, footer — whose stage is a size container, and every list divides
its own box by its rows. Under 576px of height the header goes; under 352px the
answered pawns go too. Past about fourteen players a list scrolls in its own
box, never the page — teams' trays and the track's steps too, which no row
count divides.

**The map's reveal keeps a floor on the console**, the wall's excepted:
`min(max(50cqi, 16rem), 80cqi, 42cqh)` — never flatter than the world (2:1; a
flatter frame draws ocean beside it), 16rem where that stays under 5:4, never
past 42% of the stage. What the table said gives way first, in whole chips;

### Measured traps

- On the podium (a wall connected) the round on now is a paper card read at
  arm's length: `--stage-unit` is `0.6vmin` inside it and the card is
  `clamp(24rem, 100dvh - 16rem, 34rem)` tall, so the running order shows under

  are redeclared there, because they freeze on `:root`.
- A podium column is `position: relative`: react-aria's visually hidden
  inputs inside it otherwise took the viewport as containing block, grew
  `html` to 1347px at 900, and focusing one scrolled the bar under the menu.

- The console's phase rules (`.host-console-page:has(> .stage.…)`) set the
  page's `display` at a weight no single class outranks, so the narrow panel
  hides the stage behind a tab by its id (`#main`).
- `.countdown` is the countdown token's class wherever it is drawn: a strip
  named that inside the panel came out as a 400px disc. The strip is
  `.countdown-choice`.
- A `position: fixed` element inside a page is fixed to the page, not the
  viewport: the entrance animation leaves a `transform`. Anything fixed renders
  beside `<main>`.
- `height` on the console's `<main>` does nothing: it is a `flex: 1` item of
  the app shell, and that is what holds it to the viewport.
- A folded `Disclosure` keeps its contents laid out under
  `content-visibility: hidden`; an overflow scan skips what
  `checkVisibility()` says nobody can see.
- A size container with `flex: 1` answers its queries against a zero-height box
  in Chromium. Use `flex: none` and an explicit height.
- A size container whose height a `min-content` grid row decides answers its
  queries against an earlier, shorter pass: the reveal's map at 229px matched
  `height < 152px`. Query a box the page sizes instead — the stage.
- A framed screen must not run the page entrance: its leftover `translateY` is
  a 1px scroll.
- An `auto` track centred by `justify-content` inside a size container is sized
  to its content, which is nothing: the reflex board was drawn 0px wide. The
  spec now fails a row whose contents run past its sides.
- `scrollWidth` never rises for text overflowing an `overflow: visible` box; a
  spill check must read the text element (`.nickname`) as well as its row.
- A framed page decides it is lying down by `framed.lying-down`, never a bare
  `aspect-ratio` media query: in the podium the window lies down while the
  card in its middle column stands up (1024 and 1440 both), and the tiles ran
  under the chrome. The podium wraps the seated round in `.framed-box`, a size

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is physical and shallow: a printed piece sits a few millimetres proud of
the board. The edge is a **hard** offset shadow in chipboard, not a blur; a
soft `--shadow-*` layer underneath only grounds it. Nothing floats, nothing glows.

### Shadow Vocabulary
The edge's depth is `--edge` (0.1875rem, 3px at the default size, on a piece
and a card), and every move reads it twice — the shadow's offset and the
travel — so the two stay in step at any root size: lifted, the edge grows by
three quarters of itself as the piece rises as much; pressed, it sinks by three
quarters and a quarter stays showing.
- **Piece at rest** (`0 var(--edge) 0 0 var(--chipboard), var(--shadow-piece)`):
  every die-cut piece (`piece` mixin).
- **Piece lifted** (edge × 1.75, `translateY(calc(var(--edge) * -0.75))`, with
  `var(--shadow-lifted)`): hover, fine pointers only (`pressable`).
- **Piece pressed** (edge × 0.25, `translateY(calc(var(--edge) * 0.75))`, with
  `var(--shadow-pressed)`).
- **Card** (`0 var(--edge) 0 var(--chipboard), var(--shadow-card)`): paper,
  never pressed (`card` mixin).
- **Coral piece** (`--edge: 0.25rem` in `--primary-edge`, over
  `var(--shadow-raised)`, lifted and pressed by the same ratios): the `filled`
  button and the buzzer take their own darker coral as the edge.
- **Recess** (`var(--shadow-recess-shallow)` to `var(--shadow-recess)`, with
  `inset 0 0 0 2px var(--socket-line)` or a 2px dashed outline in it): a socket,
  a disabled control, a dead buzzer; a paper text field takes the
  `--shadow-field` well.
- **Pulled forward** (`0 0 0 3px var(--spine), 0 9px 0 0 var(--chipboard),
  var(--shadow-pulled)`): the chosen lid on the shelf.
- **Overlay** (`var(--shadow-overlay)`): the menu's popover, the one thing
  that leaves the board.
- **Sheet** (`var(--shadow-sheet)`, cast upward, over `--scrim`): the same
  leaflet below the split, rising from the bottom edge.
- **Keyline** (`inset 0 0 0 3px var(--ink)`): *this one is yours* — the own seat,
  the line in hand, a selected strip segment while disabled.

**The Ruled, Never Dimmed Rule.** A state never lowers opacity. Disabled is
the empty socket, its label at full text contrast; a gone seat is muted ink plus
its `AwayMark` tag; a spent tile is a socket.

## Shapes

- **Radii**: `--radius-xs` 3px (the sprue nubs),
  `--radius-s` 8px (small controls), `--radius-m` 12px (pieces,
  controls, tiles, chips), `--radius-l` 18px (cards, lids, the room-code
  token), `--radius-full` for round tokens. Round tokens (countdown, payout,
  place, floor clock) are `card` at 50%.
- **Spacing** is `--space-*` only — 4, 8, 12, 20, 32, 48, 72 — floors and caps
  of every fluid `clamp()` included. A size set outside a typography register
  is a `--text-*` step: 0.75, 0.8125, 0.875, 1, 1.25, 1.5rem.
- **Lines**: `--stroke-hair` 1px, `--stroke-thin` 2px (the board's rule, a
  keyline, a recess ring, a dashed socket), `--stroke-bold` 3px (an ink outline,
  the *yours* keyline). `--hairline` is the board's rule whole, declared again
  by `box-lid` beside the `--rule` it reads.
- **Sprue nubs**: two 10×3px notches top and bottom centre, cut in the colour of
  the ground the piece sits on (`--ground`, defaulting to `--board`; a piece on
  paper sets `--ground: var(--card)`). They use `::before`/`::after`; a piece
  needing those passes `$nubs: false` (buttons do).
- **Lid keyline**: a 2px `--card-rule` border inset `--space-s`, radius
  `--radius-l - --space-xs` (`box-lid`).
- **Tile marks** (`TileMark`): circle, triangle, square, diamond on a 24-unit
  grid, floated at the start of the tile's first line.
- **The pawn** (`Pawn`): head, body, base on a 24×28 grid, `fill: var(--pawn)`
  over a 1.5px `--chipboard-dark` drop. The head is the piece's second cue,
  measured on the round one (centre 12, 6.5, diameter `d` 11): the **square**
  is 0.86 `d` a side with corners of 0.12 `d`, on the same centre; the
  **pointed** head is an upright triangle 1.02 `d` tall and 1.1 `d` wide, its
  apex 0.56 of its height above the centre and its base 0.44 below, grown
  0.07 `d` all round with rounded corners — the outline a stroke in the
  piece's own colour gave the approved mock-up, drawn as a fill so a dashed
  outline follows it too (its apex stands half a unit over the box, which the
  SVG lets show). All three heads are drawn and the one worn shown, so a seat
  changing its head fades from one to the other, and the fill eases from one
  colour to the next. An absent pawn is the same silhouette dashed in `--socket-line`
  inside a socket, and a socket's pawn is always round. Drawn under 13px
  wide (`0.8125rem`, read off `.standing`, a container one pawn wide), the
  head runs into the body at 1x and 11–12px read as a blot, so the track
  draws a **dot** instead: the head alone, grown from 11 units to 16 about
  its centre and sat on the base at 19, in the same `--pawn` over the same
  drop and keeping its shape — a round, a square or a triangle dot — the
  `+N` count beside it unchanged. The square and the triangle are two of the
  four tile marks too, and a head is still never read as one: a pawn beside an
  answer keeps its body and base, the reveal's answer lines print no tile
  mark, and the head alone stands only in a crowd on the track (checked on a
  full quiz reveal at 1440×900 and 390 wide). At
  1440×900 a hundred-square track draws 36px cases: a pawn alone stands
  21.6px, two 16.6px, a crowd of three or four 12.2px, and only the crowds
  are dots. The phone's strip never draws one under 13.2px (a 2.75rem case ×
  0.3), so no silhouette anywhere is smaller than the threshold.
- **Danger and good stamps**: rectangular blocks, trimmed to cap height.

## Components

### Buttons (`Button`, `Link`, `ToggleButton` — `_control.sass`)
- **Shape**: a piece. Sizes `small` (40px, `--radius-s`), `medium` (52px),
  `large` (72px), labels in `title` trimmed to cap height so only the centre
  moves. `large` sets its label on a steeper clamp
  (`clamp(1.375rem, calc(var(--stage-unit) * 3.3), 2.25rem)`) so it never falls under `medium`'s.
- **`filled`**: the coral piece, *go*. One per moment.
- **`outlined`**: a plain piece on `--track-a` in `--board-ink`.
- **`underlined`**: no piece, a printed line — 2px underline offset 5px in
  `--ink-muted`, `--ink` on hover or press.
- **Disabled**: the empty socket the piece would sit in — no fill, no edge, a
  2px dashed `--socket-line` die-line and a shallow recess, the label in
  `--ink-muted` at full text contrast. A flat fill read as one more slab in the
  dark palette; the ground showing through cannot.
- **Pending** (`data-pending`): the label goes `color: transparent` and the
  spinner paints over it in `--on-control`, so the button keeps its size and
  its accessible name.

### Answer tile (`AnswerTile`)
A pressable piece in its ink (`--tile`), `--tile-ink` text 700, the shape mark
in `--tile-mark`. `touch-action: manipulation` so a press lands with the thumb.
Once chosen, the chosen tile keeps its ink and the other three go flat into a
socket. On the console the same faces are printed, not pressable
(`PrintedChoices`, an `<ol>`).

### Question card (`_question-card.sass`, `AskedQuestion`)
Paper, `--radius-l`, the prompt in Atkinson 700 sized by the square root of its
length, the category (quiz rubric) above it. The round's clock lies along its
top (`.card-clock`, `RoundProgress`), its seconds in `--card-ink`; the card's
top padding is the clock's row. The reveal is the card turned over: the answer printed on the same
paper, a blind test's cover mounted on it.
Atlas's *name* round mounts its map on the card the way the quiz mounts a
flag: the country filled from the screen's own outlines, or a dot for a town,
framed with its neighbours above the four names or the field.

### Text field (`TextField`)
A paper slot: `--card` ground, `--card-ink`, inset shadow plus a 2px `--edge`
ring, 52px — never taller than the button that submits it. Invalid: a 3px
`--danger` ring and a stamped `--danger` block under it.

### Strips (`SegmentedControl`, `ToggleGroup` — `_strip.sass`)

### Waits: `NoticeCard`, `Loader`, `Spinner`
**A wait is a paper card, never an anonymous spinner.** Every player-side wait
(host away, round not running, …) is a `NoticeCard` (`player-round.tsx`): a
`card` lying face up, the title in `billboard` sized down, an optional detail in
`body` / `--card-muted`. The reflex wait is the same card under the reflex lid's
saffron band, with no groove — this round must show no clock.

The order of preference before any spinner: draw what is already known; say it
in words; reserve the box and let content arrive (cover art). *Known* means
known: the console's stage stays empty until the first snapshot, because an
invitation drawn from the address bar was only right for a lobby without teams —
a reload mid-round, or a wall whose table sits down in teams, flashed it before
the real stage swapped in. The wall's pairing title waits for its code the same
way, rather than sitting centred and jumping when the code lands.
`Loader` — a turning ring and a **required** named line — is the last rung,
today the cold load of a route. It waits 250ms before appearing and mounts its
`role='status'` empty, filling it after, so a screen reader hears the change.
`Spinner` is decorative geometry, only ever composed.

A verdict line above a field reserves its height and mounts empty (`.banked`
holds `1.75em` of `caption`). A wrong guess in a retry mode is `--ink-muted`,
never `--danger`: proof the frame landed, not a warning.

### App menu and connection
`AppMenu` is an icon piece fixed to the corner, inside the stage's max width,
on every screen at every phase; it carries the exits a round must keep in
reach and, while a round is framed, *give it away* at its head. The connection
sentence takes no room on screen: its `.alert[role=status]` is announced and
never drawn, and while the socket is not open `.app-menu` carries
`data-connection` and the trigger turns saffron (`--spine-reflex` on
`--on-spine-light`) with its dot pulsing — the words are the menu's first line.
Under the theme, *Install Mêlée* is an outlined row shaped like the wall
door where the browser holds an install dialog, a muted caption with the
Share glyph naming Safari's two steps on an iPhone or iPad, and nothing
anywhere else — the front door promises no install, so this is offered and
never pushed.
Under the name, a seated screen carries *My pawn* (`MenuPawn`) at every phase:
a disclosure row printing the pawn and its colour's word, opening on the same
twelve as the press. A seat in a team reads *My team's pawn* and a line saying
the whole team changes with it; while a round plays the twelve sit at half
opacity over *It changes between two rounds, not during one*, since the server
refuses a recolour then, and a refusal prints in the field-error stamp in the
line's place. The wall and a console only running the room carry none.
At its foot, small centred caption lines read as one footer, tightened against
each other: *Made by Adrien Lacourpaille*, the name linked to his site in the
reader's language, and *Source code* on every screen, then the credits at a
front door, the legal notice everywhere but its own page, and the build. A link
out of the site opens a tab of its own, so no room's socket closes for one.
On the wall the chrome goes idle
(`data-idle`) and returns on the first pointer, key or focus.
`ConnectionStatus` tells its four states by shape and motion, never by hue.

### Leaflet: popover or sheet
`Leaflet` is what a trigger opens — the menu, a seat's move. At or past the
split it is a popover beside the trigger; below it, a sheet rising from the
bottom edge across the whole width, its controls held to a 30rem column, its
top a grabber a thumb pulls down (past a quarter of its height, or flicked).
A press on the scrim or Escape closes it too. Below the split a box hung from
a top corner is out of a thumb's reach and too narrow for the host's menu,
which ran to a scrolling 520px card. Both faces are `PopoverSheet` from
`@adrienlcp/react-aria`, given the split (`WIDE_SCREEN_QUERY`) and the
rulebook's material: the package holds the overlay, the rise, the grabber and
their keyframes (`styles/_sheet.sass`), `leaflet.sass` the look. The sheet is a
plain modal with its own rise, not react-aria's `Sheet`, which enters through
the browser's smooth scroll and takes over half a second.

A sheet pads by the page's own gutter, never
the board track's: the track widens `--gutter-left` and `--gutter-right` on
`:root` to keep the page clear of it, and a sheet covering the track inherited
56px a side at 390 — 278px of controls out of 390 — for an edge it hides.

### Handing the console over
The room's screen never shows a control, so there is no switch making a
console the room's: the console is handed to another screen instead (*Run the
table from another screen*, a disclosure under the menu's wall doors: the
square on a white card at the invitation's menu size, a muted line under it,
*Copy the link*, never the address printed — it carries the token), and the
screen that handed it over becomes the wall. A console that took over from the
wall keeps every answer folded behind *Voir la réponse*.

On the wall, the board track renders beside `<main>`: inside it, the page's
leftover transform would run it down a lobby long enough to scroll. The board fills in behind its
top row, so a scrolling lobby passes under the cases rather than through their
gaps.

### The wall's composition (`wall-page.sass`)
**The wall picks its shape by its ratio, never by its width** — it is never
held in a hand, so the console's split at `$wide-screen` says nothing about it.
Taller than wide (`aspect-ratio < 1`), the reveal and the final board are one
column across the width the track leaves: the answer and what the table said
over the standings or the teams' trays, the winner over the best seats and the
board — Petit Bac's sheet included; only Atlas keeps the console's own upright
column, its map first. A square wall (up to 13/10) cannot stack an answer over a
table of teams: there the trays stand in one packed column beside it and what
the table said is one chip a row. Lying down the wall keeps the console's
split, the race on a wider column (1.35fr / 1fr). A narrow wall (810×1440)
keeps its standings under the answer and draws no score track.

**Every block stands in the middle of the box it is given**: the answer and
its chips are one block, the title and the race another, and on a standing
wall the two are centred together. On the wall the chips and the standings
are sized off the stage unit and the standings' width (`container: standings /
inline-size`) instead of dividing their box by their rows: a few chips stretched
over a full height were set at the top of an empty field, and on a 1080×1920
panel the podium stood in a third of the width while the bottom half of the
screen stayed blank. The ruled board's rows are still held to a share of the
screen's height (`--standings-height`), so a full table fits.

**A name breaks between words and nowhere else.** A podium step clamps its
name to two lines and ends a word longer than the step with an ellipsis —
`overflow-wrap: anywhere` cut a two-word name into four lines on a portrait wall. The best seats under a winning team do the same. Two pawns stand on two steps
and one on one, rather than beside an empty step, and a Petit Bac seat's name
yields its width (8rem) before a category does.

### The table (`TableLobby`)
A room of teams in its lobby, on the wall — direction D, chosen 2026-10-22
(`.impeccable/directions/board-lobby/`, local).
The track is already up, every team's pawn on the start. Centred inside it: a
**lid** (`box-lid`) with the evening's name — *Soirée en équipes*, or the one
game's — how it is won, the line-up as spines in the order the box deals them,
and the invitation laid on the lid's own grid (`display: contents`): the code
token and the words beside it on the last line, the square down the right
edge at up to 30 `stage-unit`; under 34rem of lid, the square on the left on
11 parts of 20, the code and the words stacked beside it.
Under the lid, the seats no team holds yet in a dashed recess, then one tray
per team. Before the deal the trays are empty recesses with their band — the
arrivals above already say why; a team left empty after it shows one dashed
place. Names wrap inside their tray, never under an ellipsis.

The lid and the arrivals keep a 72rem column; the trays widen past it by 15rem
a team, to the U's width. Measured at 1440×900 and 1920×1080 with 15 seats in
3×5 and 5×3, and 24 in 6×4, before and after the deal: nothing scrolls. The
code is 9.5 stage units on the lid — `.room-invitation` declares its own size,
so the lid sets it on the invitation, not on itself. A query written on the
lid answers its parent, and one written on a piece of the invitation answers
the invitation, which is `display: contents` there and never matches — so the
lid passes both sides their sizes through custom properties.

**The wall never scrolls, at any size.** The page is held to the screen and
the seats give way: the arrivals and every tray cut their own chips whole
(`useWholeChips`, which also fits again when a seat arrives or is renamed),
and a list that cut any ends on a `+N` pill, the board's crowd count. They
share their column in `minmax(0, auto)` rows, never a flex column: a list
hiding chips gives the others room and never takes it, where a flex item
shrinking by its size handed the trays' cuts to the arrivals and back.

The seats' column under 30rem is a phone held up as the wall, where 24 names
cannot all be read: the arrivals drop to `--text-xs` and the height of their
line, a tray is its band, its pawn, its name and how many sit at it (each seat
reads its own team on its own screen), and before the deal the empty trays go,
the arrivals' heading already saying a draw is coming. At 390×844 with 24
seats, the lid and six names over `+18`; after the deal, four trays two by
two. Lying down under 36rem tall (a phone lying down) the lid takes the wider
side (1.6fr) and the seats the other, under the menu's corner, the header
going as at a reveal this short; the square follows the height down (30
`stage-unit`) and the lid's rows close up. Measured at 844×390, 740×360,
667×375 and 932×430: the lid fits, four to seven names.

Under 34rem the code is set to its column, never broken: four of its widest
letters stand 2.98 times their size beside the token's `--space-xs`, which is
26px at 390 wide beside a 124px square. Kept: nobody reads a phone-sized wall
from across the room, square or code, and the table gathered round it reads
both; the square stays the largest object.

It carries no control: it is the wall's alone. The draw, the team count and
the games are the console's.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** read every colour from a token, in both palettes; a literal colour in a
  component breaks one theme silently and nothing type-checks CSS.
- **Do** keep `@layer reset, tokens, base, components` declared in
  `index.html`: a layer's position is fixed where its name first appears.
- **Do** make every control a piece (`piece` + `pressable`) and every read
  surface a card (`card`); print on paper by setting `--ground: var(--card)` or
  using `box-lid`.
- **Do** style state from react-aria's `data-*` attributes, hover only under
  `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`.
- **Do** turn any label that would frame a heading into a `VisuallyHidden`
  prefix of that heading.
- **Do** name which of two ellipsised texts in a row shrinks: a name is atomic
  where a title is not, so the payload is `flex: 1` and the name carries a
  `max-width`; a state word beside a name is atomic and the name ellipsises.
- **Do** carry a row's state as a word in `label`, with the ink seconding it.
- **Do** leave ranks and scores out until `hasAnybodyScored` says there is
  something to rank.
- **Do** run `e2e/choice-fits.spec.ts` after touching any round screen, and
  break a new check on purpose once.
- **Do** write duration references as `var(--transition-*, 0s)` — never a
  literal fallback — so reduced motion survives a missing token.

### Don't:
- **Don't** repaint the board for a phase; ink the round's marks.
- **Don't** put a kicker label above a heading. The quiz category is the only
  visible line above a question, because the table reads it.
- **Don't** let a question and its choices scroll, at any of the sixteen
  viewports, or shrink a question under 14px to get there — repair the content
  instead (the 275-char choice became 123).
- **Don't** tell anything apart by colour alone.
- **Don't** dim with opacity; rule, recess or mute the ink.
- **Don't** set danger as tinted text; stamp it.
- **Don't** use near-black grounds, neon accents, glows, gradients, or a period
  pastiche.
- **Don't** animate anything inside a running round except the clock.
- **Don't** invert the QR card in the dark palette.
- **Don't** give a framed screen the page entrance, or a size container
  `flex: 1`.

## Motion

One authored moment per event, all of it collapsing under
`prefers-reduced-motion` (`@adrienlcp/styles/reduced-motion.css`; every
duration falls back to `0s`). `--ease-out`, the shared default
`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`, is a piece set down, fast then settled, never
bounced. Durations:
`--transition-fast` 140ms, `--transition-base` 260ms, `--transition-slow`
520ms.

- **Page arrival** — `page-lay`, carried by the `screen` and `stage` mixins
  (`arriving`): the page is laid down top to bottom, a feathered mask sweeping
  down while it settles 8px, on `base` × 1.5. The mask staggers the blocks in
  reading order with no per-child delay, so a block mounting later — inside a
  round — never inherits an entrance. Filled backwards only: a mask left in
  place clips every shadow past the page's edge. Never on a framed screen.
  A page held through a round is held by `layout.arrived`, a zero duration,
  never `animation: none`: lifted at a reveal or a buzz, `none` gave the root
  a fresh `page-lay` and laid the whole screen down again, the player's at
  every reveal and a wide console's at every buzz. `none` stays only where
  the arrival is put off until there is something to lay down.
  The host's panel slides in whole from the edge it is docked to
  (`panel-dock`, its own width and a fade on `slow`, the side read from
  `--panel-arrival-from`): a rem of travel read as the panel cutting in. Off
  the desk the tab bar docks the same way from its own edge — the foot
  standing up, the side lying down — held off it until the room's first view,
  where it stood a beat before the stage it serves. No
  scale and no overshoot: a page is set down, not dropped. The page being
  left lifts off rather than vanishing: every router navigation is a view
  transition (`navigateDefaults` in `app-shell.tsx`) whose old root fades on
  `base` (`page-leave`) **stacked above** the new root, which is shown as is
  — its own `page-lay` mask is the entrance. The browser draws the new picture
  on top, so without `z-index` the fade was hidden and every page change was a
  cut. The panel, the stage and the board's cases take transition names of
  their own only during a layout move (`data-layout-move`, set by
  `moveLayout`): named on a page change, each faded in on the browser's
  default instead of arriving. The desk keeps the scrollbar gutter for as long
  as a page change runs, because the viewport changing under it makes Chromium
  skip the transition. The console's panel stands from the first frame
  (`tabs={null}` before the room's first view), its column kept and itself held
  off the edge (`data-waiting`, the animation paused): drawn only with the
  view, it pushed the invitation 330px across a 1440px desk, and slid in empty
  it had its tabs cut in under it a beat later. The stage waits for that view
  the same way: empty, it is not laid down (`:has(> .stage:empty)`), so the
  room arrives once — panel, stage, menu and board together. The waiting desk
  takes the same two children as the room's, or the first view mounts the
  page again. The menu rises with every page it is chrome for (`page-enter`) —
  standing from the first frame, it was there before the page was laid down —
  and on a room's screen, console, seat or wall, it waits for the room's first
  view, or fades in a second later on a socket that never answers. A board
  already up when a screen arrives fades in with the page, in place
  (`data-arrived-with-screen`, `swap-in` on `base` × 1.5): drawn at once, it
  stood before the stage. A screen replaced inside one page plays as a page
  change (`replaceScreen`): the seat form fades over the lobby being laid
  down, and a room's loader over the room — rendered by `main.tsx` rather than
  by the router, whose swap cut it out. The console that closed the room keeps
  its page until the page change takes it home, rather than cutting to the
  refusal that answers it.
- **A block arriving later** — on a screen already laid down. New content
  rises with `page-enter` on `base`: the player's hold, *Ready* press and
  standings a beat after the card (`slow` × 0.6, with the payout), the game a
  countdown opens and the inner clock's target with its first tick, the
  next-round card, the paused card, the wall's word that the host is away,
  and each Petit Bac category put to the vote — its sheet keyed on the
  category, so the next is laid down rather than its rows rewritten under the
  last one's head. Lifting a pause shows the round again out of
  `display: none`, which restarts its entrances: the sheet left mid-vote is
  laid down again, and that is the intended return. A game's end sets its pawn
  down first (`pawn-lands`, `fast` × 5) — the winner's on the final board,
  each player's own beside its place — with the name or the rank rising
  `fast` after it and the line under `fast` × 2; the player's final standings
  follow a beat later (`slow` × 0.6), as at a reveal. A line, a chip or a
  press taking another's place fades alone (`swap-in`, `fast`): an answer
  sent, a half banked, a slate item, the standings and hold bar beside a
  struck card or a final podium, what a phase adds to the console's header,
  the presses of a reveal or a final board, an error line under a field or a
  door, the home page's resume link, the invite page's not-found, a spinner
  (under its turn), and on the host's setup a hint, a status, the board's
  value, the line-up's stamp and the held note. A seat joining
  a roster or a tray is set down from above (`seat-arrives`, `slow`) — on the
  Players tab too, the first seat included, in the lobby only: a row the
  ranking moves would replay it. The Players tab's count is stamped on
  (`tick-stamp`, `fast`). The reconnecting pill rises a beat late (`slow` ×
  1.5), and the menu's trigger turns saffron and its dot pulses on the same
  beat, so a socket that answers at once — a seat's first — never flashes
  them, and the pill fades out on the line it last said once the screen is
  back.
  On the host's setup, a block a choice brings in rises with `page-enter` — a
  switch, strip or field a setting reveals, the blind test's strands, genres,
  decades or address form, Petit Bac's count or fixed list, the board's rules — and
  stands still when it was already there as its page or tab opened, which is
  the entrance (`useArrivedLater`); a row the host adds to a key, a label
  list, the challenge lines, Petit Bac's own categories or the kept line-ups
  rises the same way, once (`data-arrived`). A round's page opened before the
  room has echoed its entry slides in empty and its content rises as it
  lands, and the game tab's list slides back in from the side its pages leave
  by (`evening-list-in`, `base`). A
  placeholder standing before the room's first view holds the arrival
  (`animation: none` while it stands) so the page is laid down once, over the
  room. Not the player's round chrome: it remounts between framed and
  unframed phases, and a fade on mount would blink at each.
- **A panel tab opening** — `swap-in`, a fade alone on `fast` × 1.5:
  the panel is beside the game, and must not pull the eye off it. The switch
  is a layout move (`moveLayout`), so the tab left fades into the one opened
  inside the panel's own picture rather than cutting out. Off the desk a tab
  is the whole screen: it is laid down like a page (`arriving`) under the
  screen left, which fades over it (`replaceScreen`). The tab
  being left is hidden on `[data-exiting]`: react-aria keeps an exiting panel
  mounted until every animation on it settles, the entry fade included, so a
  quick switch stacked both tabs.
- **The map's zoom** — a press or a wheel notch glides 200ms on an ease-out
  cubic (`use-eased-map-view.ts`), each notch building on where the last was
  going; a finger, a pinch and a trackpad pinch land at once. Reduced motion
  jumps.
- **A piece in life** — lift under a pointer, sink under a press, on `fast`.
  Nothing else moves a piece. A spine going back into its socket, a lid's
  star and the menu trigger ease their colours on the same `fast` rather than
  snapping.
- **Pieces set out** — the home page's spines and lids arrive one after the
  other as it opens (`piece.shelved`, keyframe `piece-shelve`): each slides
  1.25rem in from its row's start with a fade on `slow`, the first a `fast`
  in and each next one `fast` × 0.3 later. The shelf staggers ten and a row of
  lids four — a 1440px row shows four lids whole and cuts the fifth — and the
  rest land together, so a piece off screen never holds back one on it.
- **Countdown** — each second struck onto the token (`countdown-strike`,
  `base` × 3.5).
- **Reveal** — `card-strike`, scale 1.06 to 1 on `slow`, on the card and never
  the screen holding it (`reveal-panel.sass`, `player-round.sass`).
- **The standings moving** — after the strike (`slow` × 3 of delay, time to
  read the podium first), a seat that changed places travels from its old row
  on `slow` × 1.5 (`standing-moves`,
  `scoreboard.sass`); a climber passes over the rows it overtakes. The order
  before the reveal is the screen's memory (`useStandingBeforeTheReveal`), so
  a reload on a reveal draws the board in place. One column only. Team trays
  travel the same way, measured rather than counted in rows: trays are of
  uneven height and deal across columns, so each starts from the box the tray
  now holding its old place sits in (`use-rows-travel.ts`, Web Animations on the same
  tokens).
- **The running order reordered** — after a drop, every row slides from where
  it was drawn when the order was sent to its new place, on `base`. A finger's
  drop holds the rows where it left them until the new order is drawn (two
  seconds at most, then they step home), so nothing steps back for the round
  trip. react-aria writes a reorder to the DOM a commit late, so both wait for
  the rows to stand in the new order (`when-rows-drawn.ts`) before measuring.
  A row arriving or leaving, as a round opens, moves nobody.
- **The board coming and going** — switched on, each case comes in from the
  edge it lies along, a wave from the start to the finish (`track-arrives`,
  `slow` with `base` of spread), while the stage draws in to make room: the
  clearance is read off `--board-presence`, a registered number the root
  transitions on `slow` × 1.5. Switched off, they go back out finish first
  (`track-leaves`, `base` with `fast` of spread) and the stage spreads on the
  same total; the track stays mounted until every animation under it has
  settled (`use-track-presence.ts`). A screen arriving on a board already up
  draws it in place.
- **The board re-laid** — a new length under a track already standing is one
  layout move (`moveLayout`, started by the track itself, so the wall moves
  the way the console does): every case both lengths share travels to its new
  cell, the finish travels with the end of the course (it is keyed as the
  finish, not by its number), and the stage slides to its new gutters. A case
  only the new length has is set down where it stands (`board-case-laid`,
  scale 0.6 to 1 on `base` × 1.25, a `fast` beat late), and a case it dropped
  is picked up on `fast`.
- **A seat back at the table** — its pawn under *Next* is stamped on
  (`tick-stamp` on `base`) as it stands up from its outline.
- **The payout** — the player's token set down a beat after the card
  (`payout-stamp`, scale 1.4 to 1 on `base`).
- **A mark earned** — a box's tick and its order badge, and a copy's check,
  pressed on (`tick-stamp`, scale 0.4 to 1: `base` on a box, `fast` on a
  button). A box's paper and band follow its lift on `base`.
- **Leaving** — exits run faster than their entry: a popover leaflet reverses
  its strike at `fast` × 0.7 (the sheet face has its own), and the arrival
  notice keeps its line drawn through `notice-leave` (`base` × 0.6) before the
  region empties, as does the bar offering to undo a round taken out, its
  press dead while it goes. An exit takes its own keyframe, never the entry reversed:
  the same name with a new direction resumes the finished entry, which ends at
  once and never reports its end.
- **The round's clock** — a CSS animation (`round-drain`) whose duration is the
  server's remaining time, drained from the fraction still standing and
  re-keyed on every snapshot; absent while the host is away. One groove
  everywhere (`--groove`, deeper than `--socket` in the dark so every phase
  ink holds 3:1 along it), drained in the phase ink, with the whole seconds
  beside it: an animated `@property` (`--seconds-left`) read through a counter
  rounded up, so the digit ticks without a render. The same clock counts the
  reveal's hold, along the foot of the console's stage. Under reduced motion
  the groove holds where the last snapshot put it and the digit keeps counting
  — it opts out of the collapse through `--reduced-motion-duration`, being a
  measurement rather than motion.
- **The flip** — instant: the reflex stimulus is a colour change and nothing
  else.
- **Continuous motion** — only `Spinner` and the reconnecting dot of
  `ConnectionStatus`.

## Sound and touch

**One sound, on the console only.** `presentation/audio/buzz-cue.ts`
synthesises the buzz: two square oscillators a perfect fifth apart (294 and
441 Hz) through a 2.4 kHz lowpass, a 200ms envelope peaking at 4ms. No audio
asset. Its peak is the console's volume × 0.3, and zero plays nothing at all.
It is armed by the same press that unlocks the clip. Eight screens at unknown
volumes would be noise, so no player screen sounds. **Nothing sounds on the
reflex flip**: each device flips on its own clock, and a honk from one machine
would score the devices ahead of it as false starts. The cue is keyed on a
buzz's `atServerTime`, which a reflex round never has.

**Three haptic patterns** (`infrastructure/browser.ts`): a 30ms tick for the
press, a 100ms thud for *the floor is yours*, two knocks (45–65–45) for *it went
to someone else* — told apart by contrast, not length. Only a screen that
pressed feels the outcome. `useBuzzOutcome` hangs off `PlayerRound` and the
host console page, not the buzzer, because the reflex reveal unmounts the
buzzer before a slow player's result arrives. `navigator.vibrate` is absent on
iOS Safari, so every pattern says something the screen says too.

## Known limits

- A list past about fourteen players scrolls inside its own box.
- Sixty slate items scroll inside their own board.
- A 20-character name truncates in the console lobby's roster, and to a few
  letters on the score track of a console held in a hand.
- Below the split a reveal shows one chip per distinct answer; which player
  said what is the player's own screen's, and the points are the track's.
- A player's reveal is not held to the screen: the answer, the round's block,
  the hold and Ready fill the first viewport, and the standings scroll under
  them at a fixed size, a column of their own lying down and on a laptop. On a
  short screen the podium keeps its first alone (under 40rem of height upright,
  50rem on a laptop), and on a track the pawn's strip heads the standings.
